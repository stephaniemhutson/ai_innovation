PROCEDURE:
Phase 1: Categorization & Relevance Filtering (The "Frontier" Test)

The first step is to determine if the patent describes an AI Application (using AI to do X) or an AI Enabler (making AI itself better). My primary filter is looking for improvements to the substrate of AI (Hardware/Infrastructure) or the fundamental logic of AI (Architecture/Optimization).

Decision Logic for Categories:

Chips: Look for CPC codes starting with H01L (Semiconductors) or G06F (Electric Digital Data Processing). Keywords: Transistor, interconnect, packaging (CoWoS, HBM), systolic array, NPU, TPU, lithography, yield.

Servers: Look for rack-level or board-level innovations. Keywords: PCIe, NVLink, blade, chassis, motherboard, bus topology.

Data Center Infrastructure: Look for thermal and power innovations. Keywords: Liquid cooling, immersion cooling, two-phase cooling, power conversion efficiency (PUE), heat exchange.

Inference: Look for optimizations specifically for running pre-trained models. Keywords: Quantization, pruning, distillation, latency reduction, edge deployment, low-power states.

Training: Look for methods to build models. Keywords: Backpropagation, gradient descent, loss functions, distributed training, parameter synchronization, foundational architecture (Transformer, SSM).

Other/Unrelated: Any patent where AI is applied to a specific vertical (e.g., "AI for medical imaging," "AI for autonomous driving") unless that patent introduces a novel hardware or algorithmic architecture that generalizes beyond that vertical.

Phase 2: Semantic Analysis (Extracting the "Delta")

To determine the score, I must isolate the Technical Delta: the specific difference between the "Background" (Prior Art) and the "Summary/Abstract" (The Invention).

Analyzing the Problem (Background): I will scan the background to see what bottleneck is being addressed. Is it the Memory Wall? Thermal throttling? Vanishing gradients?

Analyzing the Solution (Abstract): I look for the mechanism of improvement.

Weak Mechanism: "Using a neural network to optimize cooling." (This is an application).

Strong Mechanism: "A micro-channel heat sink structure with varying fin density." (This is a fundamental infrastructure improvement).

Phase 3: Vector Scoring (0-10 Impact Assessment)

I will evaluate the "Technical Delta" against the four innovation vectors using the following heuristics.

1. Energy Efficiency

Focus: Reductions in Watts/Op or Joules/Bit.

High Score Indicators: Analog computing, neuromorphic spikes, adiabatic circuits, photonics, 3D stacking (reducing interconnect distance), immersion cooling techniques.

Reasoning: If a chip architecture reduces data movement (where most energy is lost), it scores high here.

2. Compute Efficiency

Focus: Increases in Throughput (FLOPS) or reductions in Latency.

High Score Indicators: Systolic arrays, parallel processing improvements, photonic multipliers, specialized tensor cores, clock speed enhancements.

Reasoning: Does this allow the matrix multiplication to happen faster?

3. Algorithm Efficiency

Focus: Mathematical or Logical optimizations reducing the amount of compute required.

High Score Indicators: Sparse matrix operations, O(N) attention mechanisms (linear attention), new activation functions, quantization (FP16 to INT8), neural architecture search (NAS).

Reasoning: Hardware usually gets a 0 here unless it is "Hardware-Software Co-design." This is strictly for how the data is processed, not the machine processing it.

4. Memory

Focus: Bandwidth, Capacity, and Density.

High Score Indicators: HBM (High Bandwidth Memory) generations, computational memory (processing-in-memory), NVRAM, SRAM density scaling, memory compression techniques.

Reasoning: AI is currently memory-bound. Innovations that move memory closer to compute or increase the pipe size are critical.

Phase 4: Magnitude Calibration (The Scoring Scale)

To ensure the 200,000 dataset is normalized, I will apply a strict bell-curve logic:

0 (No Impact): "Method for sorting emails using AI." (Application layer).

1-3 (Incremental): Minor manufacturing tweaks, routine bug fixes in logic, standard cooling fan arrangements.

4-6 (Significant Engineering): A new standard cell library, a novel interconnect topology, a 10% efficiency gain in quantization.

7-9 (Advanced/Superior):

Hardware: 2nm process nodes, hybrid bonding, optical interconnects on-die.

Software: FlashAttention, Mixture of Experts (MoE) routing, fundamentally new loss functions.

10 (Breakthrough): Technologies that redefine the physics or math of AI (e.g., the original Transformer paper, EUV lithography, Room-temperature superconductors).

Phase 5: Handling the "Unknowns" (Frontier Detection)

Since the dataset spans 8 years and includes future predictions, I will watch for Outlier Keywords that indicate unpredicted vectors of innovation:

Keywords: "Spintronics," "Optical Neural Network," "DNA Storage," "Memristor," "Reversible Computing."

If a patent uses these hardware-agnostic physics terms to solve AI bottlenecks, I will rate them high on Compute and Energy, even if the CPC codes are obscure.
